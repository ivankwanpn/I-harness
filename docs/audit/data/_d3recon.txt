audit(d3): reconcile grok's new mechanisms into the shared rows

The three re-run crosswalks landed, so the 24 mechanisms that were sitting in
single-source rows are now merged where they belong. The matrix goes from 448
rows with 24 unreconciled, to 435 rows with NONE -- every mechanism from all seven
sources is now either matched to a cross-source capability or honestly listed as
source-unique. Coverage is exact: 830 members across 435 rows, which is the 806
original mechanisms plus grok's 24 additions.

The merges are real reconciliations, not name matching. grok's three wire shapes
behind one ApiBackend joined IH's five adapters; its classifier-plus-retry-budget
joined the provider retry row and is now the only 7-source row in the model
domain; its typed event emission joined IH's and dsh's telemetry stream row; its
external OTLP export joined a row that now spans six sources; its crash handler
joined the fail-loud shutdown row; and its ACP gateway channel pair joined the ACP
client-protocol row. Fourteen of its mechanisms remained source-unique and are
kept as their own rows rather than forced into a near-match.

The three runs also recorded what the merges flatten, which matters more than the
merges themselves. The OTLP row now hides that dsh redacts at capture time while
grok drops the whole export at export time under a fail-closed privacy gate. The
installation row hides that only opencode upstream actually updates -- the fork
merely detects, cc-custom's check is a stub -- while grok is the real self-updater
with NO checksum or signature at all: integrity there is a 30-second smoke test,
which is why a non-loopback GROK_CLI_BASE_URL is ignored. And the model discovery
row has no grok member because grok compiles its catalogue in instead of
discovering one, which is a design difference rather than a gap.

One process note worth recording: the service crosswalk was read mid-write and
appeared to place only 3 of grok's 8 mechanisms with a stale coverage block still
claiming grok: 0. Forty-five seconds later it was complete at 8/8. This is the
third time this session that a file caught mid-write looked like a defect --
cc-custom's "invalid JSON" was the first. The lesson is not to trust a single read
of a file an agent is still editing.
